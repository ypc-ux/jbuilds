# YC Application Execution Checklist

## Pre-Outreach (This Week)

- [ ] **Create live demo**
  - [ ] Spin up your Docker Compose locally
  - [ ] Test the full flow: evaluate a repo → see results
  - [ ] Take a clean screenshot for email/media
  - [ ] Note the evaluation time (shows performance)

- [ ] **Polish GitHub repo**
  - [ ] Make sure README.md is front and center ✅ (you have this)
  - [ ] Verify SETUP.md has clear deployment instructions ✅
  - [ ] Check that docs/ folder is complete ✅
  - [ ] Clean up any .env example files / secrets
  - [ ] Pin the most important commits/PRs

- [ ] **Create a 1-minute pitch deck** (optional but powerful)
  - Title: "AgentGraphology — Standardizing Tool Evaluation"
  - Problem slide: Ad-hoc evaluation = slow, risky, inconsistent
  - Solution: 5-dimensional protocol + AI-powered scoring
  - Traction: Validated on 50+ repos, zero cloud costs
  - Ask: Join your team

- [ ] **Prepare talking points**
  - How long did it take to build? (~2 months)
  - Why Ollama instead of cloud LLM? (Cost, privacy, speed)
  - Who validated this? (15+ teams)
  - What's the moat? (The protocol + data flywheel)

## Finding the Right Contacts

### Research Phase (30 mins)

- [ ] **Find the company's site and funding announcement**
  - Search: "[Company Name] Y Combinator" or "YC S2026" (adjust for current batch)
  - Read the YC article (usually has founder names + context)
  - Find their homepage → Team page → Get founder names

- [ ] **Extract founder/recruiting contacts**
  - [ ] Check company careers page (sometimes has hiring email)
  - [ ] Search founders on Twitter/LinkedIn
  - [ ] Search company name + "careers@" or "hello@"
  - [ ] If nothing, look for engineering lead on LinkedIn

- [ ] **Prepare a list (aim for 10-15 contacts)**
  - [ ] Primary contact (founder, CEO)
  - [ ] Secondary (CTO, VP Eng)
  - [ ] Recruiting email if available
  - [ ] Try multiple founders (they'll forward to right person)

## Outreach Phase (Rolling)

### Day 1-2: Founder Outreach

- [ ] **Email Tier 1 targets** (founders, CTOs)
  - [ ] Use subject line: "Founder application — AgentGraphology team"
  - [ ] Personalize: "I saw you built [feature] and I'm doing something adjacent"
  - [ ] Include portfolio link + GitHub
  - [ ] Ask for 15 mins
  - [ ] Keep it <200 words
  - [ ] No attachments (people won't download)

- [ ] **Quick follow-up** (if no response in 3 days)
  - [ ] Short, friendly ping: "Wanted to make sure this didn't get buried — would love to chat 👉 [link]"
  - [ ] Don't re-send full deck, just nudge

### Day 3-5: Secondary Outreach

- [ ] **If founders don't reply, try:**
  - [ ] YC partners on the company page (if listed)
  - [ ] Other founders in the YC batch (they'll know who you should talk to)
  - [ ] Recruiting/hiring email
  - [ ] Engineering team LinkedIn profiles

### Day 7+: Parallel Path

- [ ] **While waiting for replies:**
  - [ ] Reach out to 3-5 other startups doing related work
  - [ ] Engage with YC online (Hacker News, YC forums)
  - [ ] Share your work on Twitter (good signal of momentum)

## If You Get a Meeting

### Before the Call
- [ ] **Prepare a live demo**
  - Run the app locally
  - Have a few sample repos ready to evaluate
  - Know evaluation time + architecture inside out

- [ ] **Prepare 3 questions to ask them**
  - "What's your vision for tool evaluation in the next 2 years?"
  - "Where do you see the biggest gaps right now?"
  - "What would you need from a co-founder?"

- [ ] **Dress the part**
  - Business casual minimum
  - Professional background if video call

### During the Call (15 mins suggested time)
1. **Intro** (1 min): Your background, what you built
2. **Problem** (2 mins): Why tool evaluation is broken
3. **Solution** (3 mins): Show the product, explain the moat
4. **Traction** (2 mins): 50+ repos validated, metrics
5. **Ask** (1 min): "I'd love to join your founding team"
6. **Conversation** (6 mins): Let them ask questions, listen

### After the Call
- [ ] **Send thank-you email** (within 2 hours)
  - Reference something specific they said
  - Reiterate your interest
  - Suggest next steps if any

## Timing Notes

- **Best time to email:** Tuesday-Thursday, 9am-11am (founders actually check email)
- **Avoid:** Friday (gets buried), Monday morning (inbox chaos), weekends
- **Follow-up window:** 3 days to 1 week (don't spam, but be persistent)
- **If ghosted after 2 tries:** Move on to next person (YC companies get lots of inbound)

---

## Contingency Plans

**If they say no / not hiring:**
- "Would you recommend any other YC companies or partners I should talk to?"
- Use this as networking opportunity (they might refer you)

**If they ask for more details:**
- Offer a 30-min technical deep-dive
- Share code walkthrough
- Live eval demo

**If they want to see the app:**
- Provide 1-command deployment (Docker Compose)
- Share a test GitHub URL to evaluate
- Keep support minimal (you're not building features for them yet)

**If they want to hire you as employee (not co-founder):**
- Negotiate: Early equity stake (1-5%), title (Senior Eng or Tech Lead), autonomy on decisions
- Don't accept pure salary play — you have IP/traction

## Final Tips

1. **Confidence > Desperation**
   - You built something real and tested it
   - They'd be lucky to have you on the team
   - Frame it as "let's own this together" not "please hire me"

2. **Personalization Matters**
   - Don't send the same email to everyone
   - Reference something specific about their product/vision
   - Shows you actually care, not just spamming

3. **Keep Pushing**
   - First 2-3 attempts rarely work
   - Persistence (without being annoying) is the differentiator
   - If YC batch isn't right fit, pivot to other VCs/angels

4. **Play the Long Game**
   - Even if they don't hire you, you've now:
     - Built relationships in the space
     - Validated your problem/solution
     - Have a network for future ventures

5. **Celebrate Wins**
   - If you get a meeting: you already won (they're interested)
   - If you get an offer: massive win (join the team)
   - If you get feedback: invaluable (iterate product)

---

## What NOT to Do

❌ Send generic email to 100 people  
❌ Ask for funding (you're joining, not fundraising)  
❌ Criticize their product or approach  
❌ Over-explain in initial email (save for call)  
❌ Go silent after getting their reply  
❌ Accept just because you're frustrated
